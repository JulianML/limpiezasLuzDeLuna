## Why some windows get dirty in a week while others stay clean for two months

It has probably happened to you: you clean the windows on a Saturday, they look perfect… and a few days later, with the afternoon sun, haze, streaks and little white dots appear. It isn't bad luck, and your home isn't in a particularly dirty area. In most cases, the culprit is the products the glass was cleaned with.

After more than 35 years cleaning windows across the Marina Baixa, we are certain of one thing: the quality of the products doesn't just decide how the glass looks on day one, but **how long it stays clean afterwards**. Properly cleaned glass leaves no residue, and glass without residue takes much longer to get dirty again.

## Water: the product nobody sees

The first "product" in any cleaning job is water, and here on the Costa Blanca we start at a disadvantage: tap water in the province of Alicante is very hard, loaded with limescale and minerals. When a drop dries on the glass, the water evaporates but the limescale stays behind. Those are the white marks that reappear as soon as the sun hits the window.

That is why our [professional window cleaning service](/en/servicio-cristales.html) uses **pure reverse-osmosis water**: water filtered until virtually all dissolved minerals are gone. When it dries it leaves nothing on the glass — no limescale, no salts, no marks. It is the same technique that lets us clean large windows and façades with water-fed poles from the ground, including [high-level window cleaning](/en/cristales-altura.html).

## Detergents: more foam doesn't mean cleaner

Many supermarket glass cleaners contain fragrances, shine enhancers, silicones or cheap surfactants. Freshly applied they look shiny, but they leave a thin, invisible film on the glass. That film is slightly sticky and works like a magnet: dust, pollen and airborne sand cling to it far sooner than to truly clean glass.

### What we avoid on every job

- **Products with fragrances, waxes or silicones:** they leave a haze and attract dust.
- **Too much detergent:** more soap doesn't clean better; it just leaves more residue to rinse off.
- **Ammonia and harsh products:** they can damage solar-control films, tinted glass and frame seals.
- **Vinegar on marble or stone sills:** its acidity can dull natural stone.
- **Kitchen paper or newspaper:** they shed lint and don't lift dirt properly.

We use professional pH-neutral detergents, in the right dose, always followed by a final rinse that leaves no trace.

## Tools are "products" too

A good result depends as much on what applies and removes the liquid as on the liquid itself. A squeegee with a worn rubber blade leaves streaks with every pass; a poor-quality microfibre cloth, or one washed with fabric softener, just moves grease around. That is why we replace blades and microfibres frequently and only use glass scrapers on thoroughly wet surfaces, so the glass is never scratched.

## How long do the windows in your home stay clean?

It is the question we are asked most. It depends on orientation, distance from the sea, nearby building work or muddy rain, but in our experience in the area these are approximate timeframes for exterior glass:

- **DIY cleaning with supermarket products and tap water:** the glass starts to look dirty after 1 to 3 weeks.
- **Professional cleaning with pure water and neutral products:** it usually stays clean for 6 to 10 weeks.
- **Beachfront homes:** sea salt shortens the timeframe, usually to 3–5 weeks even with professional cleaning.
- **Interior glass:** properly cleaned, it can last several months, except for high-use areas like doors or shower screens.

### What shortens the life of a clean

- **Calima:** Saharan dust arriving with the rain changes everything. Read our [Calima survival guide](/en/blog/post/calima-costa-blanca/).
- **Sea salt:** in Benidorm, Altea or Calpe, the sea breeze deposits salt on the glass day after day.
- **Nearby building work:** cement and plaster dust sticks especially well to glass with product residue.
- **Sprinklers and pools:** splashes of hard water leave limescale on the lower part of windows and glass balustrades.

## How to make the clean last longer between visits

- Dust dry with a clean microfibre cloth before dirt builds up.
- To touch up a mark, use distilled water and a drop of neutral soap — never a scented glass cleaner.
- Keep tracks and frames clean: dirt from the runners ends up on the glass.
- Schedule regular cleans: besides saving effort, [preventive maintenance](/en/blog/post/mantenimiento-preventivo-vidrios/) protects the glass from limescale and salt damage that eventually can no longer be removed.
